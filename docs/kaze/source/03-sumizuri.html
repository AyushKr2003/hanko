Sumizuri, printed in your inks

墨摺

Your wallpapers stay yours. Kaze prints them again in the theme's paper and four inks, crushed to five tones (the way Tsushima's menu art crushed its values) with a fixed paper grain. Whatever covers most of the picture becomes the paper. On a dark theme a light print turns into a rubbing (ishizuri 石摺): white marks on a black stone.

Most of it becomes paper地

The print's average brightness decides. The Great Wave and Sudden Shower are mostly light paper, so on a dark desk they flip into rubbings. The night pines and Red Fuji are mostly dark, so they keep their tones. The haboku painting is cut from its mount and floats in empty paper.

Measured: pines 0.18 · haboku 0.21 · Red Fuji 0.43 · Sudden Shower 0.59 · Great Wave 0.65. Above 0.5 the dark theme prints the negative.
No red is ever printed: red stays for "you" and "now" (Hanko rule 2), even under Red Fuji.

The sky keeps the hour空

"90% of the mood comes from your upper hemisphere" (Tsushima's creative director). The top 40% of the print lifts or settles a few percent with the sun, re-drawn every 15 minutes. You never see it change, only that the evening feels like evening.

Every theme, every print色The print is drawn by a shader from the theme's colours, so switching theme re-inks every wallpaper at once. No wallpaper ever clashes with the bar.
The season's character候The microseason tunes the print slightly: more grain and a harder crush in the cold kō, softer edges in spring. Felt, not seen. No new colour.
Off is one switch切`wallpaper: print | original`. Original shows the image as it is, with only the ground fade under the grass.
